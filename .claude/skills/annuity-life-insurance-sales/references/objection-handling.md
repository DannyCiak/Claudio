# Objection-Handling Patterns

Use a consultative sequence: validate, clarify, educate, disclose tradeoffs, and connect back to the documented goal.

## "I hate annuities."

Response pattern:

1. Validate: "A lot of people have heard mixed things about annuities."
2. Clarify: "What specifically concerns you: liquidity, fees, complexity, income, or something you heard from someone else?"
3. Educate: "There are different categories. Some are built for income, some for fixed rates, some for protection with limited upside. None is right for everyone."
4. Reconnect: "The question is whether any category helps cover your essential-income gap while preserving enough liquidity and flexibility."

## "I do not want to lock up my money."

Response pattern:

- Agree that liquidity matters.
- Separate emergency assets and near-term spending from retirement-paycheck assets.
- Discuss surrender periods, free-withdrawal provisions, income-only designs, and alternatives.
- State that a recommendation is not suitable if it creates an unacceptable liquidity shortage.

## "The fees are too high."

Response pattern:

- Ask which fee or contract cost they mean.
- Distinguish no-explicit-fee products from rider-fee products and securities-based products.
- Compare cost to the benefit purchased: income guarantee, death benefit, LTC leverage, or downside protection.
- Disclose that costs reduce value and must be justified by the client’s documented need.

## "I can just invest the money myself."

Response pattern:

- Agree that investing remains important for playchecks.
- Explain that paychecks solve a different problem: essential income that should not depend solely on market returns.
- When appropriate, explain mortality credits and sequence-risk reduction.
- Avoid saying annuities beat investments; say they can complement investments when suitable.

## "My friend/CPA/adviser says annuities are bad."

Response pattern:

- Respect outside input and invite review.
- Ask what concern they raised.
- Offer to provide a plain-English summary of the purpose, costs, liquidity, and alternatives.
- Encourage the client to include the professional in the decision.

## "What if the insurance company fails?"

Response pattern:

- Explain that guarantees depend on the issuing insurer’s claims-paying ability.
- Discuss financial-strength ratings at a high level.
- Avoid over-promising state guaranty association protections; suggest reviewing state-specific limits and disclosures.
- Consider diversification across carriers when appropriate.

## "I need to think about it."

Response pattern:

- Do not pressure.
- Ask what information is missing.
- Recap the client’s stated goals, the paycheck gap, tradeoffs, and alternatives.
- Set a specific follow-up to answer remaining questions.
